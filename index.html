Interfacial Electrochemistry

Ziqing Wang

Mechanistic interfacial electrochemistry for energy storage, conversion, and separations.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in Materials Science and Engineering at the University of Pennsylvania, working with Prof. A. Shoji Hall. My research connects electrolyte structure, charge-transfer kinetics, mass transport, and crystallography to the rational design of electrochemical systems. I received my Ph.D. in Analytical Chemistry from The University of Texas at Austin with Prof. C. Buddie Mullins.

Research

My long-term goal is to establish quantitative links between molecular-scale interfaces and device-scale performance, turning mechanistic measurements into transferable design rules for electrochemical technologies.

01

Metal-electrode kinetics and crystallography

Resolve intrinsic charge-transfer kinetics, facet-dependent reactivity, nucleation, and morphology evolution using ultramicroelectrodes, fast-scan voltammetry, SECCM, and single-crystal electrodes.

02

Electrolytes and interfaces for metal batteries

Design aqueous, water-in-organic, molecular-crowding, gradient, and high-entropy electrolytes that regulate solvation, mass transport, interphases, and metal deposition.

03

Electrochemical conversion and separations

Engineer interfacial microenvironments for CO₂ and nitrate electroreduction and develop electrically programmed strategies for selective chemical and critical-material separations.
