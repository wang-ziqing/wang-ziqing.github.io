**Current direction:** CO₂ and nitrate electroreduction in the Hall group at the University of Pennsylvania.

## The question

Electrocatalytic reactions occur within an evolving local environment, not on an isolated catalyst surface. How do surface chemistry, electrolyte composition, and transport jointly govern reactivity and stability?

## Ongoing research

My postdoctoral research focuses on electrochemical interfaces and engineering for **CO₂ and nitrate reduction**. I am interested in how microenvironmental control can connect mechanistic understanding to sustained electrochemical conversion, including the role of carbonate accumulation and electrode passivation in CO₂ reduction.

This work extends the kinetic and transport perspective developed during my doctoral research. Specific unpublished experimental strategies and results are not included here.

## Published foundations

My collaborative work includes a review of transition-metal-based water-oxidation electrocatalysts and a study of phase-boundary-rich bimetallic selenides for oxygen evolution and sodium-ion storage.
